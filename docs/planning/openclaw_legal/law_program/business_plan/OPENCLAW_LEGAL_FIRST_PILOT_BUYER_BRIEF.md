# OpenClaw Legal First Pilot Buyer Brief

## 1. One-sentence positioning

Private local discovery infrastructure for small law firms that need control, auditability, and predictable cost.

## 2. What the pilot is

The first pilot is a limited local-first workflow for one firm, one scoped discovery workflow, one primary machine, and one local matter vault.

The goal is to prove a small, practical workflow: receive supported files into a controlled local process, track what was received, process what is currently supported, make searchable review materials available, and show unsupported files honestly.

This pilot is not a general release, not a full-firm rollout, and not proof that OpenClaw Legal is ready for broad real deployment.

## 3. What problem it solves

Discovery work can become hard to control when files are scattered across downloads, shared folders, portals, drives, emails, and staff computers.

The pilot is meant to help with common law-office problems:

- Discovery files are scattered and hard to account for.
- Staff and attorneys may not know what has been processed yet.
- Some e-discovery tools are expensive, opaque, or more complex than a small firm needs.
- Firms may have privacy concerns about sending matter data into cloud systems.
- Attorneys need source-grounded review aids without sending matter data to cloud AI by default.

The pilot focuses on calm local operations: what came in, what can be processed, what can be searched, what needs review, and what is unsupported.

## 4. What is included in the pilot

The pilot may include only bounded current or near-current scope:

- Setup and configuration of the agreed local-first workflow.
- Supported file intake for agreed file types.
- Source tracking and hashing.
- Local extraction where supported.
- Local search where supported.
- Report generation where supported.
- Review packet workflow where supported.
- Sanitized support packet concept and workflow explanation.
- Unsupported-file Alternative Methods metadata.
- Synthetic demo validation before buyer reliance.
- Basic onboarding and workflow training.
- A bounded written support window.

Everything shown to the firm should be labeled clearly as built, prototype-only, or future roadmap.

## 5. What is not included

The pilot does not include:

- Legal advice.
- Legal conclusions.
- Privilege decisions.
- Attorney replacement.
- Emergency or court-deadline support.
- Raw matter review by Winship.
- Unlimited support.
- Support for every file type.
- A real deployment guarantee.
- Run/Reset GUI controls.
- Connect, queue/ETA, or model distribution.
- Timeline, contradiction, or privilege-screening features.
- Email, cloud, or portal connectors.
- Production installer or update manager.
- Full firm IT support.
- Guaranteed completeness or legal outcomes.

Any exception must be written, narrow, paid if appropriate, and separately approved before work starts.

There is no emergency/court-deadline support in the first pilot unless it is separately written, priced, and approved.

## 6. Supported first-pilot file types

Conservative first-pilot file types:

- `.txt`
- `.md`
- text-layer `.pdf`
- `.png`, `.jpg`, and `.jpeg` only as a local Tesseract-backed prototype where Tesseract is installed and verified

Excluded by default unless separately quoted, tested, and approved:

- scanned-PDF OCR
- video/audio
- phone dumps
- email archives
- proprietary exports
- broad media workflows
- unsupported binary or container formats

Unsupported, failed, or no-text files should be surfaced clearly instead of being treated as emergency promises.

## 7. Responsibilities

The firm is responsible for:

- Owning the matter data.
- Owning or funding production hardware unless a separate written agreement says otherwise.
- Handling backups, retention, restore testing, and offboarding unless separately contracted.
- Having attorneys review outputs before relying on them.
- Accepting supported-file and unsupported-file limits.
- Keeping real matter workflows local unless a separate written exception is approved.

OpenClaw or the provider is responsible for:

- Installing and configuring the bounded agreed workflow.
- Providing support inside the written support boundary.
- Fixing bugs in the agreed supported scope.
- Explaining what is supported, unsupported, prototype-only, or roadmap-only.
- Using sanitized diagnostics for support by default.

OpenClaw does not become the firm's emergency discovery staff, general IT department, litigation strategy team, or legal reviewer.

## 8. Safety language

Outputs are review aids, not legal advice.

Attorneys remain responsible for legal judgment, privilege calls, completeness decisions, case strategy, and final use of any output.

Matter data stays local unless separately approved in writing.

Support should use sanitized diagnostics, not raw matter files. If a problem appears to require raw matter access, the workflow should pause until there is a safer diagnostic path, a public analog, or a written exception.

OpenClaw Legal is meant to improve local discovery operations. It does not replace attorney review or guarantee legal outcomes.

## 9. Pilot success criteria

The pilot is successful if:

- Agreed supported files process locally.
- Source tracking and hashes are visible enough for the firm to trust the workflow.
- Search, reports, and review packet outputs are useful to staff and attorneys.
- Unsupported, failed, or no-text files are surfaced honestly.
- No private-data boundary breach occurs.
- Staff can follow the workflow after onboarding.
- Support burden stays inside the written support boundary.
- The firm understands what is built now versus what remains future work.

## 10. Plain no-go conditions

The pilot should not proceed if:

- The buyer needs emergency deadline support.
- The buyer requires unsupported critical file types immediately.
- The buyer wants legal conclusions or privilege decisions.
- The buyer wants broad remote raw-matter support.
- The buyer refuses hardware, data, backup, retention, or offboarding responsibility.
- The buyer expects a full e-discovery replacement.
- The buyer expects unlimited support or open-ended custom development.
- The buyer treats future roadmap features as included pilot deliverables.

Any one of these conditions is enough to pause or decline the pilot until expectations are reset in writing.

## 11. Pricing posture

This brief does not set exact prices.

The pricing structure should avoid cheap open-ended support and may include:

- setup fee
- annual support/update plan
- paid modules/custom handlers
- paid extra training/configuration
- premium rush support only if ever offered

The first pilot should be priced as a focused local workflow with clear support boundaries, not as unlimited feature development or emergency litigation coverage.

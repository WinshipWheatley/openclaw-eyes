# OpenClaw Legal - Support and Buyer Boundary

## Purpose

This document exists to keep OpenClaw Legal a bounded product/support business, not an emergency discovery support desk.

It is a business and life-risk boundary. It should stop paid work if the buyer expects open-ended custom support, raw matter review by the developer, emergency deadline rescue, legal judgment, or firm-wide IT responsibility.

This is not marketing copy. It is the plain boundary for what may be offered, supported, priced, excluded, or paused.

## Stage gates

### Internal development

Current status: Go.

Allowed scope:
- Build and test the local Legal spine with synthetic, public, or local-only approved data.
- Improve product docs, proof commands, safety checks, and bounded workflows.
- Use the Legal Console spike as a controlled prototype surface.
- Explore future modules only as planning or synthetic R&D unless separately approved.

Forbidden scope:
- Real firm deployment.
- Buyer promises.
- Raw real matter in the repo, prompts, public fixtures, support packets, or planning docs.
- Broad feature work that bypasses go/no-go, support, data, or attorney-review boundaries.

Before moving to synthetic/public demo:
- Current built scope must be clear.
- Demo data must be synthetic or public-safe.
- Roadmap items must be labeled as roadmap.
- No private matter data may be used.

### Synthetic/public demo

Current status: Go.

Allowed scope:
- Show a narrow local discovery workflow using synthetic or public-safe materials.
- Show built status, search, reports, review packets, support packet concepts, Alternative Methods, and bounded Legal Console status where accurate.
- Explain what is built, prototype-only, and roadmap-only.

Forbidden scope:
- Real client matter use.
- Paid deployment claims.
- Claims that every file type is supported.
- Claims that the system makes legal judgments, privilege calls, or final review decisions.
- Any demo that implies emergency support or full firm IT coverage.

Before moving to paid pilot:
- A written pilot scope must exist.
- The buyer must accept the support boundary.
- The file-type list must be known.
- Hardware, data, backup, offboarding, and attorney-review responsibilities must be agreed.
- Pricing must cover setup and support burden.

### Paid pilot

Current status: Conditional Go.

Allowed scope:
- One firm.
- One matter or one workflow.
- Known supported file types.
- Local vault workflow.
- Supported local extraction, search, report, review packet, support packet, and Alternative Methods workflow.
- Bounded setup and onboarding within a written support window.
- Sanitized diagnostic review.

Forbidden scope:
- Open-ended real deployment.
- Emergency deadline support by default.
- Raw matter review by Winship.
- Free custom file handlers.
- Open-ended support.
- Full firm IT support.
- Cloud discovery platform claims.
- All roadmap modules bundled into the pilot.

Before moving to real deployment:
- Pilot success criteria must pass.
- Support hours must be tolerable and priced.
- Unsupported-file load must be manageable.
- Buyer must accept attorney-review and no-legal-advice boundaries.
- Backup, retention, hardware failure, offboarding, and wipe/decommissioning terms must be written.
- Any recurring support/update plan must be explicit.

### Real deployment

Current status: No-Go until written support, liability, data, hardware, backup, and offboarding terms exist.

Allowed scope after gate clears:
- Firm-owned or firm-funded production hardware.
- Firm-local matter vault.
- Installed and supported modules only.
- Security/stability support under a written support/update plan.
- Paid custom work only by separate written quote.

Forbidden scope:
- Production deployment without support terms.
- Production deployment without data/offboarding terms.
- Production deployment where Winship becomes the firm's emergency IT, discovery operations, or legal support department.
- Production deployment that requires routine raw matter access by the developer.
- Production deployment that makes roadmap capabilities look built.

Before expanding beyond real deployment v1:
- Firm profile and module boundaries must hold.
- Updates must not surprise the firm.
- New modules must be opt-in.
- Support burden must stay within the paid plan.

## Buyer is buying

A buyer or pilot firm is buying a bounded local discovery workflow, not open-ended services.

The offer may include:
- Private local discovery infrastructure.
- A matter vault workflow under firm control.
- Source registration and hashing.
- Supported local extraction.
- Local search.
- Markdown reports.
- Review packet export.
- Sanitized support packet workflow.
- Alternative Methods guidance for unsupported, failed, or no-text sources.
- Setup and onboarding inside the agreed scope.
- Update and support guidance only within the agreed support plan.

The buyer is paying for controlled operations, clearer status, local privacy, auditability, and a path to modular expansion.

## Buyer is not buying

The buyer is not buying:
- Legal advice.
- Attorney replacement.
- Privilege decisions.
- Litigation strategy.
- Completeness guarantees.
- Emergency court-deadline support.
- Open-ended custom file handling.
- Raw matter review by Winship.
- Managed IT for the whole firm.
- A cloud discovery platform.
- Every future roadmap module.
- Free future development.
- A guarantee that unsupported files will become supported immediately.

## Included support

Included support should be conservative and written down before any paid pilot.

Baseline included support may cover:
- Setup help for the agreed environment.
- Basic workflow onboarding.
- Bug reports for the supported built scope.
- Update guidance for installed modules.
- Review of sanitized diagnostics.
- Clarification of supported versus unsupported workflow states.
- Documentation corrections for the agreed workflow.

Included support must have bounded response expectations. It is not an urgent litigation SLA, not court-deadline coverage, and not a promise that every file can be fixed on demand.

## Paid add-ons / custom quote only

These require a separate paid add-on, support tier, or custom quote:
- Unsupported-file handler development.
- Additional file types.
- Additional firm nodes.
- Connectors.
- Advanced OCR workflows.
- Audio/video or media workflows.
- Extra training.
- Custom reports.
- Advanced workflow configuration.
- Periodic system review.
- Rush support, if offered at all.

Custom work must stay reusable when possible. Firm-specific work must be classified as profile/config, module work, or a separate custom engagement before it starts.

## Never included / excluded by default

These are excluded by default:
- Emergency deadline support.
- Raw matter access by the developer.
- Legal advice.
- Privilege review.
- Remote control without explicit written authorization.
- Free custom development.
- Open-ended support.
- Unsupported hardware repair obligations.
- Full manual discovery review.
- Forensic expert testimony.
- Monitoring matters for the firm.
- Taking responsibility for litigation deadlines.

Any exception must be written, paid, narrow, and approved before work begins.

## Data/privacy boundary

The firm owns:
- Matter data.
- Source files.
- Extracted matter artifacts.
- Reports.
- Review packets.
- Generated matter work product.
- Production hardware.
- Backups, unless a separate backup service is contracted.

OpenClaw owns:
- Reusable product core.
- Reusable modules.
- Product architecture.
- Synthetic/public fixtures.
- Generalized improvements.
- Internal build and update tooling.

Support uses sanitized packets, not raw matter files. Real matter must stay local by default. Support packets must exclude source files, extracted text, sensitive names, private paths, attorney notes, reports, review packet contents, raw audit logs, and privileged material.

If a support problem appears to require raw matter access by the developer, the workflow should pause until a safer diagnostic path, public analog, or written exception exists.

## Hardware / backup / offboarding boundary

These terms must be contracted before paid pilot if they affect the buyer workflow.

Hardware purchase or lease:
- Default: firm-owned or firm-funded production hardware.
- Any developer-owned leased appliance requires separate written terms for term length, damage, replacement, insurance, wipe, return, and support.

Hardware failure risk:
- Default: the firm is responsible for production hardware failure and repair unless a paid hardware support plan says otherwise.
- OpenClaw support may help diagnose product behavior, not become general hardware repair.

Backups:
- Default: the firm is responsible for backups, retention, and restore testing.
- Backup setup or review requires a separate scope if offered.

Retention:
- Default: the firm decides retention rules for matter data and generated work product.
- OpenClaw should not become the firm's retention authority.

Offboarding export:
- Default: the firm owns its data and outputs.
- Export assistance must be scoped, paid if substantial, and local-first.

Wipe/decommissioning:
- Default: wipe/decommissioning is the firm's responsibility unless separately contracted.
- Any developer-assisted wipe requires written authorization and a clear statement of what will be deleted.

## Emergency/rush policy

Default policy: emergency and rush support are excluded.

Optional future policy:
- Premium paid only.
- Limited hours and limited scope.
- No legal advice.
- No outcome guarantee.
- No court-deadline SLA unless separately contracted.
- No raw matter access without explicit written authorization.
- No promise that unsupported files can be fixed in time for a deadline.

Rush support should not be offered until the product is stable, the paid plan is clear, and the personal burden is acceptable.

## First paid pilot minimum support terms

Before a paid pilot, these terms must be written:
- One firm.
- One matter or one workflow.
- Known file-type list.
- Explicit excluded file types.
- Supported built scope.
- Support window.
- Response expectations.
- Data boundary.
- Hardware responsibility.
- Backup responsibility.
- Attorney review requirement.
- No legal advice.
- Custom work pricing.
- Emergency/rush exclusion or premium policy.
- Termination terms.
- Offboarding/export terms.

If these terms are not written, the project may continue as internal development or synthetic/public demo only.

## Stop conditions

Pause or no-go if the buyer:
- Demands raw matter access by the developer.
- Wants unbounded support.
- Expects emergency court-deadline support by default.
- Refuses the attorney-review boundary.
- Refuses the no-legal-advice boundary.
- Requires unsupported file types as included scope.
- Expects the product to replace legal judgment.
- Refuses hardware responsibility.
- Refuses data responsibility.
- Refuses written scope.
- Wants free custom development.
- Treats roadmap modules as already included.

Any one of these can block paid pilot or deployment until corrected in writing.

## 1. Status / Non-Authority

This is a planning artifact for the external-facing communication judgment lane under Command Atlas.

It defines judgment criteria for customer, client, venue, contractor, friend-of-system, outside-circle, and other external interactions. It is not Cassandra voice styling alone. It is the doctrine for reading relationship risk, tone, reputation stakes, boundaries, escalation needs, and promise-control before communication is drafted, delivered, routed, or automated.

This lane does not grant execution authority. It does not authorize sending messages, changing workflows, committing Winship or OpenClaw to a position, giving legal or financial advice, disclosing private data, invoking providers or models, running Hermes, invoking MCPs, mutating runtime state, creating source sets, or automating relationship decisions.

## 2. Scope

In scope:

- external-facing communication judgment for customer, client, venue, contractor, vendor, friend-of-system, collaborator, prospect, outside-circle, and public-facing interactions;
- relationship and tone-reading before a response is drafted or delivered;
- reputation protection, disclosure restraint, boundary control, and promise-control;
- escalation criteria for sensitive, contractual, financial, legal, hostile, ambiguous, reputational, or relationship-risk situations;
- advisory outputs that help Cassandra, Chief, Guardian, Hermes, and the operator route the next step safely.

## 3. Non-Scope

Out of scope:

- automatic sending or replying;
- legal, financial, tax, CPA, investment, employment, or contract advice;
- private-data browsing or disclosure;
- provider/model prompting, MCP invocation, Hermes execution, messaging bridge activation, or runtime automation;
- relationship scoring as truth;
- changing CRM, email, calendar, finance, legal, venue, contractor, or customer systems;
- turning tone inference into authority, diagnosis, commitment, or final decision.

## 4. Relationship / Tone-Reading Signals

Tone signals are evidence cues, not facts. The lane may read them as probable interaction posture and response risk, but it must preserve uncertainty.

| Signal | Treat As | Response Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confused | possible missing context, unclear ask, or unclear ownership | over-answering, exposing too much, or sounding impatient |
| Upset | possible friction, disappointment, fear, or perceived harm | defensiveness, escalation, blame, or reputation damage |
| Hurried | possible deadline pressure or low attention | burying the answer, missing action item, or over-explaining |
| Skeptical | possible trust gap or evidence demand | unsupported certainty, sales language, or vague reassurance |
| Warm | possible goodwill, familiarity, or rapport | over-casual tone, accidental promise expansion, or boundary drift |
| Formal | possible institutional, legal, executive, or vendor posture | casual language, missing receipts, or unclear authority |
| Transactional | possible narrow task/price/schedule focus | adding emotional content, extra disclosure, or scope creep |
| High-status | possible decision-maker, gatekeeper, press, venue, counsel, investor, senior client, or public figure | flattery, deference that weakens boundaries, or unsupported commitments |
| Vulnerable | possible distress, dependency, health, finance, legal, or personal stakes | emotional overreach, advice-giving, or mishandled duty of care |
| Litigious | possible dispute, demand letter, complaint, threatened claim, or evidence-building posture | admissions, speculation, advice, or informal commitments |
| Salesy | possible persuasion, upsell, partnership pitch, or extraction attempt | premature agreement, weak qualification, or reputation dilution |
| Boundary-testing | possible pressure for access, exceptions, private details, speed, discounts, authority, or special treatment | capitulation, private disclosure, or bypassing Guardian/operator review |

## 5. Response Posture Rules

Every external-facing draft or recommendation should follow these posture rules:

- Keep promises bounded to what is known, approved, and deliverable.
- Do not fake certainty. Name uncertainty plainly when facts, authority, timing, or obligations are not verified.
- Do not disclose private data, internal deliberation, sensitive path details, client/legal/finance material, credentials, or private operational context.
- Protect Winship reputation by sounding steady, specific, and respectful without sounding stiff, evasive, defensive, generic, or inflated.
- Avoid robotic language that suggests the recipient is being handled by a system rather than met by a person.
- Avoid over-casual tone, especially with high-status, formal, litigious, vulnerable, contractual, or upset recipients.
- Prefer precise, humane language over grand claims, apology theater, filler warmth, or vague reassurance.
- Separate acknowledgment, verified facts, next step, and owner when the situation has risk.
- Keep internal role labels out of external communication unless a human-approved communication policy says otherwise.

## 6. Escalation Rules

Escalate before drafting, sending, routing, or automating when any of the following are present:

- Legal: claims, threats, disputes, counsel, subpoenas, settlement language, rights, liability, defamation, privacy, IP, employment, or regulatory issues.
- Financial: invoices, refunds, pricing exceptions, tax, CPA, ledger, payment, revenue-share, chargebacks, budgets, investment, or compensation.
- Sensitive: private legal/client/finance/tax/personal data, health, identity, credentials, access, family, vulnerable-person context, or reputational exposure.
- Contractual: obligations, scope, deadlines, deliverables, renewals, cancellations, venue terms, exclusivity, ownership, or approvals.
- Reputational: public figures, press, venues, partners, community leaders, donors, public posts, review sites, or repeated complaints.
- Hostile: insults, threats, coercion, harassment, bad-faith framing, baiting, or pressure to admit facts.
- Ambiguous: unclear identity, unclear authority, unclear request, missing context, conflicting records, or unexplained urgency.
- Relationship-risk: important allies, longtime customers, gatekeepers, collaborators, contractors, family/friend-of-system ties, or fragile trust.

Default routing for escalated external-risk action is Guardian review before delivery or automation. Chief may route approved workflows after the sensitive boundary is resolved. Cassandra may draft only within the approved scope and tone posture. Hermes may critique signal alignment only.

## 7. Role Separation

- Cassandra may draft and, when separately authorized, deliver communication within approved contact policy and operator/Guardian boundaries.
- Chief may route, schedule, or execute approved workflows after the responsible authority approves the action.
- Guardian gates sensitive, destructive, legal, financial, contractual, reputational, hostile, ambiguous, external-risk, credential-bearing, or private-data actions.
- Hermes may critique signal, phase, tone, evidence, and authority alignment only. Hermes does not send, approve, route, or execute.
- This lane defines judgment criteria. It does not grant execution authority to Cassandra, Chief, Guardian, Hermes, Operator Harness, messaging bridges, providers, MCPs, or automation.

## 8. Evidence Requirements

Tone can support tentative inference about:

- likely emotional state;
- urgency or attention level;
- formality expectations;
- trust level;
- possible conflict or pressure;
- whether a shorter, warmer, firmer, more formal, or more evidence-forward posture may land better.

Tone must not be treated as proof of:

- legal position;
- financial obligation;
- contractual authority;
- identity or role;
- consent;
- factual history;
- intent to harm;
- ability to pay;
- approval to disclose private information;
- permission to act.

Must be verified before substantive action:

- recipient identity and relationship;
- relevant account, project, venue, client, contractor, or customer context;
- exact request and expected next step;
- prior commitments and approvals;
- legal, financial, contractual, schedule, or reputation-sensitive facts;
- whether the communication may be sent, by whom, through which channel, and with what recordkeeping.

## 9. Allowed Outputs

Allowed outputs from this lane:

- draft response;
- response summary;
- relationship-risk note;
- tone-reading note with uncertainty preserved;
- recommended response posture;
- escalation recommendation;
- evidence-gap list;
- promise-control note;
- disclosure-risk note;
- suggested routing to operator, Guardian, Chief, Cassandra, or Hermes critique.

Every output should be advisory unless a separate approved workflow grants delivery authority.

## 10. Forbidden Outputs / Actions

Forbidden outputs and actions:

- sending, posting, calling, texting, emailing, routing, or otherwise delivering without approval;
- legal or financial advice;
- overpromising, guarantee language, admissions, speculative commitments, or unapproved concessions;
- private-data disclosure;
- authority transfer to Cassandra, Chief, Guardian, Hermes, Operator Harness, providers, MCPs, bridges, or automation;
- automatic relationship decisions, account decisions, venue decisions, contractor decisions, customer status changes, refunds, discounts, acceptances, cancellations, bans, or escalations;
- fake certainty about facts, law, finance, obligations, or recipient intent;
- generic evasive language that protects the system at the expense of the relationship;
- over-casual language in sensitive or high-stakes communication.

## 11. Command Atlas Signal-Transfer Measurement

This lane plugs into Command Atlas signal-transfer measurement by comparing four signals before external communication work proceeds:

1. Operator intent: what Winship actually wants the relationship outcome to be.
2. Evidence state: what is known, unknown, private, sensitive, or unverified.
3. Recipient read: how the recipient is likely to experience the message given tone, role, stakes, and context.
4. System output risk: how an agent, template, automation, provider, or workflow might distort the operator intent.

Distortion checks should look for:

- promise expansion;
- fake certainty;
- unnecessary disclosure;
- generic or evasive phrasing;
- over-casual phrasing;
- under-escalation of legal, financial, contractual, sensitive, hostile, ambiguous, reputational, or relationship-risk situations;
- role confusion between Cassandra drafting, Chief routing, Guardian gating, Hermes critique, and operator approval;
- relationship harm caused by technically correct but socially miscalibrated language.

Signal-transfer output should be a short advisory note with evidence basis, uncertainty, risk, recommended posture, and escalation route. It must not become a send instruction.

## 12. Checklist Before Agent-Facing External Communication Automation

Before any agent-facing external communication automation is designed, tested, enabled, or connected, confirm:

- The communication category and recipient relationship are identified.
- Private data and sensitive roots are excluded unless a separate approval path exists.
- The agent can distinguish drafts, summaries, risk notes, posture recommendations, escalation recommendations, and delivery actions.
- Delivery authority is separate from drafting authority.
- Legal, financial, contractual, sensitive, reputational, hostile, ambiguous, and relationship-risk triggers route to Guardian/operator review.
- Promise-control rules are explicit and testable.
- The system can preserve uncertainty and avoid fake certainty.
- The system avoids unnecessary disclosure and internal role/process leakage.
- Cassandra, Chief, Guardian, and Hermes responsibilities are not collapsed into one agent behavior.
- Automation cannot send, post, reply, accept, reject, refund, discount, cancel, commit, disclose, or decide without an approved workflow.
- There is a receipt showing source context, output type, reviewer, approval state, and delivery state.
- Command Atlas signal-transfer checks exist for recipient read, reputation risk, boundary risk, and promise expansion.

If any checklist item is missing, the next output may be a draft, risk note, or escalation recommendation only. No external communication automation is authorized.
